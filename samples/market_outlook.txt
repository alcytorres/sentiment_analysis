Weekly Market Outlook — October 2025

U.S. equity markets ended the week mixed, with the S&P 500 gaining 0.4% while the Nasdaq slipped 0.2%. The divergence reflects ongoing rotation from growth into value names as investors digest mixed economic signals. Treasury yields rose modestly, with the 10-year settling at 4.35%.

The Federal Reserve minutes released Wednesday reinforced expectations for a cautious approach to rate cuts. Several committee members emphasized that inflation, while trending lower, has not yet reached the 2% target on a sustained basis. Market pricing now implies one additional 25bp cut by year-end, down from two cuts priced in last month.

Labor market data was broadly constructive. Nonfarm payrolls came in at 185,000, slightly above expectations, though the prior two months were revised down by a combined 35,000. The unemployment rate held at 3.8%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month-over-month, keeping wage growth in a range that is neither alarming for inflation nor signaling labor market weakness.

Corporate earnings season is underway, with about 30% of S&P 500 companies having reported. So far, 72% have beaten earnings estimates, roughly in line with the five-year average. Revenue beats are running slightly below average at 58%. Financials have been a standout, with major banks posting strong trading revenue and improving credit quality.

Key risks to monitor include the upcoming CPI print, escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, and signs of consumer spending fatigue in discretionary categories. Housing starts and existing home sales data next week will provide further insight into the rate-sensitive sectors of the economy.

Overall, the near-term outlook is cautiously constructive. The economy is growing, earnings are holding up, and the Fed appears to be in no rush to tighten further. However, valuations in some corners of the market leave little margin for disappointment.
